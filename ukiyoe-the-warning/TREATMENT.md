# Three Sheets, One Bay — Ukiyo-e

**Logline.** An old woman warns a fisherman off the sea. The warning, the voyage and the storm are printed on three sheets that only make one picture when they are seen side by side, and the camera crosses them without a cut.

> **Revision note.** My first treatment was a five-view series with a block-by-block opening, handscroll slides, a hard cut to silence and a wave that breaks out of the border. Comparing it with the library demo (`styles/ukiyoe/DEMO.md`) showed it matched on four or five of the six counts (structure, opening, signature shot, camera, score shape), so I rebuilt it. What follows is the film as delivered.

## Three candidate structures
1. **Five views of one bay** — the obvious reading of the style (constant subject, five prints). Rejected: it is the demo's own structure.
2. **A print being made** — a carver's blocks, one per shot. Rejected: the storm has to *happen*, not be manufactured.
3. **A triptych** — one scene split across three sheets joined by narrow mount gaps: *Morning* (the warning on the shore) | *Noon* (the boat, a brown sky, clouds stacking) | *The Storm*. **Chosen.** The story is a horizontal pan, and the whole only reads when the three sheets are seen together.

**Arc.** Setup — an extreme crop of woodgrain pulls back to find the first sheet; the old woman points at a small dark cloud (0–15 s). Escalation — slow pans to the noon sheet and then the storm (15–35 s). Peak — the wave, a flash of lightning, one ringing shamisen note, the crash, and a crash-zoom into the woodgrain, so the medium itself becomes the image (35–46 s). Ending — the camera pulls back to the three sheets; the old woman's lamp glows on the first sheet; the paper washes white and only a red seal remains (46–60 s).

## Benchmark
Learn: *Thirty-six Views* (one place composed three ways, bold cropping), *Hundred Famous Views* (rain as two sets of fine lines, tiny figures), triptych prints whose scene runs across sheets. Don't take: any specific print or composition.

## Differences from the demo (checked after the treatment, per DIRECTOR.md §11)
| | Demo | This film |
|---|---|---|
| Structure | five views, one constant subject | triptych, one scene across three sheets |
| Opening | printing block by block | extreme woodgrain crop pulling back |
| Signature shot | wave breaks out of the border | crash-zoom into the grain at the peak (frame stays intact) |
| Camera | handscroll slides between prints | two continuous pans + a final pull-back |
| Score shape | jo-ha-kyū with a hard cut to silence | accelerating taiko, then one ringing shamisen note (no silent cut) |
| Ending | quiet resolution, scale reveal | lamp on the first sheet, wash to paper, seal alone |

## Cue map and sound design
Taiko heartbeat 60→132 bpm across 12–38 s; shamisen ostinato 22–37.9 s; clappers accelerate 28.5–31 s; roll 35.6–38 s; shamisen A3 rings alone 38.0–39.6; the crash lands on 39.6. Shakuhachi breaths open and close the film. Ambience: surf; rain bed 25.5–46 s; wind; crickets in the last scene. Foley: baren rub for the woodgrain crop, wood ticks for cartouches, scroll slide for each pan, seal stamp at the end.

## Subtitle and title design
Horizontal cream cartouche (thick+thin ink border, red seal at left) in Shippori Mincho; view titles in vertical cream slips with a pale tab and Yuji Syuku type, printed as part of each sheet.

## Native moves used
Triptych · crash-zoom into the texture · paper is the white (the final wash) · printed seals · bokashi skies · flat colour blocks printed slightly out of register (the storm sheet lands out of register at the crash and settles).
